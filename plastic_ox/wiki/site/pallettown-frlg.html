Story notes

Act 1 · 0 — Opening · full · drafted
Design source: plastic_ox/story/locations/pallet-town.md

Snapshot

A small seaside town of low roofs, white fences, and a laboratory that is far too big for it. The player wakes here, walks to Oak's lab, and chooses between Treecko, Squirtle, and Cyndaquil. This is the whole tutorial: no convergence explanation, no Rocket rumor, no Bill. The only strangeness allowed is that three professors are in one room and none of them can fully explain why.

Plot points

  1. Oak sends the player to the lab. Short, warm, ordinary. The player has no assignment yet beyond "go see what Oak wants".
  2. Three professors, one table. Oak (Kanto), Elm (Johto), and Birch (Hoenn) are together, each presenting one starter. Elm and Birch are visiting, not resident. Nobody monologues about the merged world; the professors are excited about a research problem, the way academics are excited about grants, not apocalypses.
  3. Starter choice is permanent and quiet. The player picks Treecko (left ball, Birch), Squirtle (center, Oak), or Cyndaquil (right, Elm). The two unpicked balls stay visible for the rest of the game.
  4. Pokédex assignment. Oak asks the player to record what they find, not to save the world. This is reveal-ladder step 1: the world converged, stated once, plainly, and then dropped.
  5. Supplies, then the road. Five Poké Balls, the Exp. Share handoff from Elm, a catching tip from Birch, and Oak's go-ahead. Elm leaves for his lab and Birch for the field; Oak stays.
  6. The water edge is a wall. The southern beach is fenced off with the starter gate: without a starter the player is turned back with a plain "dangerous to leave without a Pokémon". Pallet's southern water relationship to Cinnabar is visible geography, never a route.

Must not: name Bill, Team Rocket, Silph, the adaptive system, or "something is still changing the world". The first hour should read as a normal Pokémon opening that happens to have three professors in it.

Dialogue guidance

  • Oak is busy and kind, speaks in short declaratives, and never explains more than he is asked. He is the only person in town who is visibly tired.
  • Elm is anxious about being a guest; he overpacks, thanks people twice, and talks about Cyndaquil like a nervous parent.
  • Birch is outdoorsy and loud, treats the lab like a greenhouse, and is the only one who says "Hoenn" without lowering his voice.
  • Mom is unimpressed by professors and deeply interested in whether the player has eaten.
  • Neighbours know the lab has visitors and are deciding how to feel about it: proud, nosy, mildly inconvenienced by the parking.
  • Keep sentences under two lines per textbox. Pallet should sound like a place where people have known each other for thirty years.
SIGN LADY: Three professors in our little town. I keep expecting one of them to ask me for directions.
FAT MAN: A machine that records every Pokémon you meet. I'd lose mine in a week.
MOM: Eat something before you go. The professors can wait; they're used to it.
BIRCH: Trecko's been restless since we got off the boat. Might be the sea air.
  • Do not: have any NPC say the words "convergence", "merged region", or "Kanto, Johto and Hoenn" in one breath. Do not foreshadow the Champion.

Inspiration

  • Pallet as a real seaside village: a Maine or Cornish harbour town in late spring — cold water, white paint, one café, everybody knows the ferry schedule. The lab is the only building with air conditioning.
  • FireRed/LeafGreen Pallet's rhythm: a bedroom, a short walk, a lab, one choice. The remake's Pallet is quiet because the game trusts silence before the first route.
  • Academic-visit comedy: three specialists in one small lab, competing politely over desk space and the one good chair. This is where the humour lives — not in jokes about the map.
  • Dialogue conversion: give each neighbour a job related to the visit (catering, laundry, the inn, the ferry) so the professors' presence is felt through logistics rather than exposition.

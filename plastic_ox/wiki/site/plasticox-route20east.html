Story notes

Act 3 · 10 — Seafoam passage · full · drafted
Design source: plastic_ox/story/locations/plasticox-route20east.md

Snapshot

The eastern half of the Seafoam passage: open water from the cave's east mouth to Mossdeep's western beach. The player surfaces at (6,14), crosses 47 tiles of empty sea, and steps onto Mossdeep at (53,10). There is nothing here — no object, no trainer, no encounter table — and that is the point: after the ice and boulders, the route is a held breath before Mossdeep's Gym and Space Center.

Plot points

  1. Out of the cave, not around it. The only entrance is the Seafoam warp at (6,14) (Seafoam 1F warp 4). The map declares no connections on any edge; the sea beyond the lane is MB_OCEAN_WATER with no surfable exit, so the "no surf-around" rule holds by geometry rather than by a visible obstacle.
  2. The exit is a story gate. The coord at (53,10) runs PoxRegion_sea_mossdeep_a: it warps to MossdeepCity (0,21) once FLAG_POX_STORY_MANSION is set. Before the Mansion it refuses and puts the player back at (52,10) — which is one tile west, so the trigger can fire again on the next step east. Normal play never sees this, because Route 20 West already requires the same flag.
  3. Reveal-ladder step 10 is a gap, not a scene. Seafoam is the exploration breath between Cinnabar's archives and Mossdeep's Space Center. Nothing here advances the mystery; the route must not add a lore NPC or a washed-up clue.
  4. The sea is empty on purpose. Zero objects, zero trainers, zero wild or fishing entries, and no signs. If encounters are added later they belong to the Seafoam floors, not this lane; surf fishing here would undercut the directed-dungeon design.
  5. Mossdeep is next. The landing at (0,21) is the town's west beach, in front of the Gym 6 and Space Center chapter. The route's job is to deliver the player there with no business in between.

Must not: name Bill, Silph, the adaptive system, or the Space Center findings; add a Rocket, a shipwreck, or a researcher; put an item or a trainer on the water; explain why the sea is closed.

Dialogue guidance

  • There is no one to talk to, and that is a deliberate contrast with the crowded Cinnabar and the staffed Space Center. Do not add a talker to make the map feel busy.
  • If a voice is ever added, it should be a working one at the edge of the passage — a diver checking a marker buoy, a ferry spotter on the Mossdeep beach, a fisherman who cannot believe the water is this empty. One line, and it should be about weather or work, never the plot.
  • The register is wind, distance, and a little boredom. Short lines if any.
DIVER (proposed): Two hours down and the only thing I found was a boot. Not mine, either.
FERRY SPOTTER (proposed): You came out of the cave? Then you're the first thing I've seen all morning.
FISHER (proposed): Not a nibble since the water changed. I keep coming out anyway. It's quieter than the harbour.
  • Do not: give the sea a mystery, a survivor, a message in a bottle, or a legendary silhouette. Seafoam and Mossdeep carry those beats.

Inspiration

  • Hoenn's open-water routes (127–129): long blue lanes where the map itself is the encounter, and the relief of land is the reward. The difference here is that Plastic Ox keeps the lane empty, so the relief is Mossdeep.
  • Real ferry passages: the half-hour between two islands where the deck is cold, the water is loud, and nobody speaks. That silence is the texture.
  • Diving and marker buoys: orange floats, a channel marker, a bell buoy — visual furniture that says "shipping lane" without a single NPC.
  • Lighthouse logic: Mossdeep's Space Center should be visible on the horizon as a white line before the player lands; the route exists to make that approach feel earned.
  • Dialogue conversion: if the map ever gets a voice, it is a job on the water — a diver, a spotter, a fisherman — and the topic is the day, not the world.

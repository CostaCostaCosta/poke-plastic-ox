Story notes

Act 3 · 9 — Lavender / Team Rocket II · full · drafted
Design source: plastic_ox/story/locations/lavender-town.md

Snapshot

A hillside town of tiled roofs and narrow stairways, quieter than its size suggests. The Pokémon Tower stands above it with its door open and its candles lit; two Rocket grunts are inside, asking the wrong questions of the wrong man. The player rescues Mr. Fuji, is pointed at Cinnabar, and leaves through Route 8 while the southern harbour road stays shut behind them.

Lavender is the game's pivot from travelling through strangeness to investigating it: Rocket is no longer stealing things, it is asking the same questions the player has been asking since Ecruteak.

Plot points

  1. Arrival is quiet. Fortree → Route 110 → Lavender. No cutscene at the town gate. The player should notice the tower before anyone explains it.
  2. Team Rocket II — Pokémon Tower. Two grunts occupy the tower and are interrogating Fuji about teleportation, preservation of living states, and old Cinnabar research. This is the second of exactly three Rocket encounters. They are frightened of what they found, not triumphant.
  3. Fuji is rescued and points to Cinnabar. He recognises the kind of research he is being asked about but not the cause. He names the Cinnabar archives and, crucially, the practical route: Route 8 → Underground Path → Route 7 → Route 103. The directions are geography, not lore.
  4. Reveal-ladder step 7. Rocket is investigating the phenomenon. Fuji confirms the investigation has a paper trail. Do not reveal the system, Bill, or Silph here.
  5. Saffron stays sealed. Lavender's west road is the way around the closed city, never through it.
  6. South is postgame. The harbour road is visibly closed, with people standing at the barrier. After the Champion battle it opens to the Battle Frontier ferry. Do not spend the south exit on the Cinnabar transition.
  7. The tower is a place of grief, not a dungeon. NPCs lower their voices. The player is a visitor here before they are a hero.

Must not: have Fuji name the cause; have Rocket monologue about a master plan; open the harbour early; show Bill anywhere near this.

Dialogue guidance

  • Lavender's public voice is hushed. Short sentences, no exclamation marks. People describe the tower the way you describe a church: by its hours, its candles, who is buried there.
  • Mr. Fuji is gentle, tired, and precise. He does not explain more than he knows, and he is ashamed that his old field is being mined. He calls the grunts "those men", never "Rocket".
  • Rocket grunts are rattled. They expected a haunted tower and found a filing cabinet. Their lines should be questions with no good answers: "How do you move a person without moving the room?"
  • Mourners and caretakers carry the town's emotional weight. One person should be there for a Pokémon that died long ago; one should be there out of habit.
  • Harbour people are bored and practical. The barrier is a schedule problem, not a mystery.
  • After Fuji is rescued, the town's register changes: people are relieved but not celebratory.
TOWER CARETAKER: We keep the candles low. The Pokémon here don't like a bright room.
GRUNT: You think we wanted this? We were told to find a man, not a library.
FUJI: I spent a life learning how to keep things. I never learned how to put them back.
HARBOUR CLERK: Ferry's not running. Hasn't been since the roads moved. Come back when you're famous.
  • Do not: use "convergence", "adaptive system", "Silph" or "Bill" here. Do not make the tower a jump-scare level; the horror is bureaucratic.

Inspiration

  • Lavender as a real Japanese temple town: stone stairs, grave plots on a hillside, a shop that sells incense and soda from the same cooler. Sound: wind chimes, a distant bell, a scooter idling.
  • Pokémon Tower in RBY/FRLG: the silence before the first wild encounter, the Channelers who speak in fragments, the feeling of intruding.
  • Ghost-story pacing: the tower should be read, not explained — a knocked chair, a candle that leans, a radio playing in an empty room.
  • Dialogue conversion: give the town three social roles — caretaker, mourner, harbour worker — and let each talk about the tower from a different distance. That is the whole town in three voices.

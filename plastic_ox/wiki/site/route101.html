Story notes

Act 1 · 0 — Opening network · full · drafted
Design source: plastic_ox/story/locations/route101.md

Snapshot

A twenty-by-twenty-tile strip of bright Hoenn meadow pinned between Pallet's north fence and Oldale's south gate: the first place the player may walk into grass. No trainer, no gate, no cutscene — two children, one sign, seven species in the weeds. Its job in the badge spine is to make the player catch something before Route 29 asks them to fight with it, and its emotional note is permission: the game stepping back and letting them wander.

Plot points

  1. The first step out of Pallet. The connection down to PalletTown_Frlg (offset -2) leaves the player at the town's north fence. Nothing checks them here; the only gate in the opening is Pallet's beach gate. Reveal-ladder step 1 was spoken in the lab and this route adds nothing.
  2. Grass is the first verb. Wild table gPoxRoute101 holds Wurmple, Poochyena, Taillow, Lotad, Seedot, Surskit and Slakoth at Lv. 2–4. Lotad and Seedot in one patch — a version pair in Hoenn — is the quiet evidence that the world is stitched; the player should notice the mix, never hear it explained.
  3. The youngster is the tutorial. At (16,8) Route101_EventScript_Youngster says one Pokémon was not enough and the trainers ahead are strong. He means Route 29's two trainers; there is no trainer on this map. He is the replacement for the "first trainer battle" the human todo list asked to delete.
  4. The boy at (2,13) has crossed a seam without noticing. A Poochyena followed him up from Pallet; he asks whether he smells different to the Pokémon here. A joke about bodies, not about geography.
  5. The sign at (5,9) only points forward. "ROUTE 101 / OLDALE TOWN". Nothing points back to Pallet; the player is expected to keep walking.

Must not: name the convergence, Bill, Silph, Team Rocket or any system; place a rival, a cutscene, a forced battle or a gift; imply the player is chosen; promise trainers on Route 101 that do not exist.

Dialogue guidance

  • Only two people speak here, both children. Their world is Pallet behind them, Oldale ahead, and whatever they can see from the path.
  • Youngster at (16,8) is eleven or twelve and repeats adult advice with total confidence. Orders, one clause per box. He does not change after the player wins anywhere.
  • Small boy at (2,13) talks about his own body and about animals, never about regions. Short sentences and one question he cannot answer.
  • The sign is the only institutional voice on the map: plain, faintly proud.
  • Register: bright, loud, unembarrassed, out of breath — a village footpath on a warm afternoon. There is no before/after shift, and that is the point: nothing happens here.

Inspiration

  • RSE Route 101: the shortest route in Hoenn, the first grass, the zigzag ledges, a game that hands over the controls without instructions. At 20×20 this version doubles as the seam between FRLG Pallet's white fences and ORAS Oldale's pastel hedges.
  • A real footpath between two villages in late summer: cut grass drying on a verge, a dog that follows you a mile, school bags by a gate — and the sound of cicadas, a bicycle freewheel, someone calling a kid in for lunch. Sun, no wind; the school holidays' last hour.
  • Conversion: two social roles only — the child who repeats an adult warning, the child who asks a physical question. Overheard topics: animals, shoes, lunch. Never maps.

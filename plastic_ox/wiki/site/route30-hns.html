Story notes

Act 1 · 0 — Opening network · supporting · drafted
Design source: plastic_ox/story/locations/route30-hns.md

Snapshot

A reservoir road that used to be the way north out of Cherrygrove: a lake at the top, a lane bending past it, two houses that take in walkers, and five people with reasons to stand still. It awards no badge, and that is the point — here the widened world shows up as traffic, water levels and a full kettle, not as a story. The map is currently orphaned (audit below), so this note is the design for the route as it should read once the seam is restored.

Plot points

  1. The lake, then the lane. The player comes up from Cherrygrove with the water on one side and the hill on the other. The road visibly continues past the lake; nobody treats that as remarkable. This is the route's whole introduction and it should take ten seconds.
  2. Two houses with two jobs. The rest house at (26,39) is a bench, a kettle and a woodpile; the surveyor's house at (35,4) is a front room full of old notebooks. Both are reasons to knock, not quest stops.
  3. The local chorus. Don counts bugs, Joey brags about a Rattata, the woman at (20,13) times her day by the Cherrygrove market, and the boy at (26,31) counts lorries. They are the first NPCs in the game who are simply busy; let the player overhear rather than interview.
  4. No reveal. Reveal-ladder step 1 (the opening world) was spent in Pallet and is this route's ceiling. A resident may notice that the lane is busier and that a sign points somewhere they have not been; nobody connects it to anything larger.
  5. Seam audit — the map cannot be entered. Route30_hns declares up to Route31_hns and down to MAP_CHERRYGROVE_CITY_HNS, but Cherrygrove's north edge now belongs to Route2_Frlg, and Route31_hns declares no south connection back. Nothing warps in and the only reciprocals are the two house doors. A debug warp that lands the player here strands them at Route31_hns if they walk north.

Must not: explain why the lane exists, promise a visitable Violet City, or put Rocket, Bill, Silph or a region theory in anyone's mouth. Do not add a climbable shortcut to Route31_hns to "fix" the seam.

Dialogue guidance

  • The boy narrates vehicles and water levels. He is the only person on the route who talks to the player first; keep him at one idea per line.
  • Joey is a small-town competitor: confident, specific, faintly ridiculous. He should never say the word "region" twice in a conversation.
  • The woman works; her sentences are about prices, ice and the ferry, and she is the one who mentions Cherrygrove without being asked.
  • Don speaks in field notes: dates, weather, page numbers. The joke is the guidebook, not the Pokémon.
  • The old man is unhurried and slightly deaf; the surveyor is precise and embarrassed that his instruments lie.
  • Register: short declaratives, no exclamation marks except the boy's. If the seam is restored after Ilex, these people talk like commuters; before Ilex, like neighbours.
BOY: Two lorries before lunch. Neither stopped. That's how you know it's a real road now.
JOEY: Top percentage of this route. It's a small route. It still counts.
DON: Rain on Tuesday, sun on Wednesday, and a bug on Thursday I've drawn on the back page because there isn't a front one for it.
OLD MAN: Kettle's on. The bench is dry if you're quick about sitting.
SURVEYOR: Every hill on this map is forty metres off where I wrote it. I've stopped blaming the ink.
  • Do not: use "convergence", name Bill/Silph/Rocket, explain the lake as unnatural, or let anyone say "Violet City" as if the player can go there.

Inspiration

  • Johto Route 30 in GSC: the lake, the fork, Mr. Pokémon's kitchen-table research, and the feeling that Johto's routes are lived-in before they are dangerous. Convert the kitchen table into the surveyor's front room and the lake into a working reservoir with a valve house.
  • A reservoir lane in the Japanese hills, rainy season: frogs in the overflow channel, gravel washed onto the tarmac, a tarp over a woodpile, one vending machine at the rest house. Jobs: reservoir keeper, bait and ice seller, part-time post runner.
  • The two households as small businesses: a tea-and-bench rest stop and a retired surveyor who takes in odd jobs. Their talk is about customers, deliveries and the cost of ice, which is how the widened world should arrive here.
  • GSC's Route 30 theme (the slow Johto route music): long gaps between events, a screen of tall grass, one trainer who is a kid. Let the silences do the work; this is the route to walk, not to clear.
  • Slice-of-life pacing (a summer-vacation comic, not an adventure one): observations are the plot. The boy's lorry count and Don's back page are the equivalent of chapter titles.
  • Conversion: give every NPC a route-adjacent job — water, ice, post, surveying, school run — and let each mention one inconvenience caused by traffic. No one needs to mention the map.

# Pokémon Plastic Ox — Main Story Outline

**Version: 0.4**

## Narrative Goal

**Pokémon Plastic Ox should feel first and foremost like a traditional Generation I–III Pokémon adventure.**

The player explores a merged world, catches Pokémon, earns eight Gym Badges, encounters familiar places and characters, resolves a growing technological mystery, and ultimately challenges the Pokémon League.

Desired pacing:

**Crystal-style restraint → Emerald-style escalation → traditional Pokémon League finale**

The Gym Challenge is the narrative spine. The mystery interrupts that journey rather than replacing it.

Version 0.4 preserves the central mystery, reveal ladder, and Champion Bill payoff while changing two major pieces of the late game:

- **Whirl Islands replace Seafoam Islands** in the first major water region.
- **Route 41 replaces Route 20** so the Whirl Islands retain their native overworld context.
- **Bruno becomes the eighth Gym Leader**, battling the player in Saffron's Fighting Dojo.
- The official **Saffron Gym is present but inaccessible** during the main story.
- The exact reason the official Gym cannot be entered is intentionally **TBD**.
- Sabrina is therefore no longer part of the required eight-Gym progression.
- The existing v0.3 physical-port changes remain in force: Mt. Moon/Route 4 removal, Route 115 NU branch, Dark Cave network, Route 118 adapter, Route 121 late-game junction, Hoenn ocean chain, Route 11 Saffron approach, and Route 23 Victory Road approach.

---

# Core Premise

Kanto, Johto, and Hoenn have inexplicably converged into one region.

Towns, routes, ecosystems, research facilities, and infrastructure that once existed far apart are now connected in impossible ways.

Professor Oak, Professor Elm, and Professor Birch are meeting in a new starting town formed from elements of:

- Pallet Town
- New Bark Town
- Littleroot Town

They do not know what caused the convergence.

The player is not a chosen hero. The professors simply need Trainers to explore the changed world, document habitats, and report anything unusual.

Immediate goals:

1. explore the region
2. complete the Pokédex
3. earn eight Gym Badges
4. challenge the Pokémon League

The mystery develops gradually.

---

# The Central Character: Bill

Bill is the hidden central figure.

His technology should feel like one continuous progression:

**Pokémon storage → teleportation → biological fusion/separation → Time Capsule compatibility → regional storage networking → battle-state collection → adaptive battle intelligence → AI-driven Trainer development → regional convergence**

Silph and Devon supply infrastructure, hardware, power, and research support. Bill is the architect of the underlying technological lineage.

---

# Bill Before the Plot

Bill should be mentioned long before the player suspects him.

Early references establish that:

- he created the original Pokémon Storage System
- he is from Goldenrod
- he is obsessed with Pokémon
- he loves Eevee
- he has historically been a better researcher than battler
- he disappears into projects for long periods

The player should first know Bill as:

> **the strange genius who built the PC system**

not a mysterious mastermind.

---

# Bill's Core Motivation

Bill knows extraordinary amounts about Pokémon biology, transfer, storage, and behavior, yet he has never been considered an elite battler.

That contradiction fascinates him.

His question becomes:

> **What separates someone who knows everything about Pokémon from someone who can consistently win battles?**

He begins collecting:

- battle states
- teams
- moves
- switches
- outcomes
- Trainer decisions
- opponent responses

He develops a system whose original purpose is:

> **Learn what makes the strongest Trainers win.**

Eventually Bill begins using it to train himself.

---

# Act I — Strange Things Are Happening

## Opening

The professors explain only that the regions have merged.

Do not:

- introduce AI
- say Bill is missing
- announce that the Champions are missing
- frame the player as uniquely destined to solve the crisis

The player begins a normal Pokémon journey.

---

# Opening Journey — Before Gym 1

Progression:

**Merged Starting Town → Route 29 → Cherrygrove → Route 30 → Route 31 → Rustboro**

Optional exploration:

- lower Route 46 from Route 29
- early Dark Cave pocket from Route 31

Cherrygrove has no major plot event.

NPCs can react to impossible geography or unusual habitats, but nothing should imply a hidden mastermind.

### Gameplay purpose

Everything before Roxanne is the **Little Cup phase**.

The player should have enough Little Cup-legal species to construct a deliberate team. The tier structure is communicated through gameplay rather than central-story exposition.

---

# Rustboro — First Major City

Devon Corporation is introduced as normal regional infrastructure.

The player can learn that Devon:

- manufactures Pokémon technology
- supports Trainers and researchers
- maintains systems used across Hoenn
- collaborates with regional technology networks

A Devon researcher may provide a mundane field device or diagnostic utility.

At this stage **Devon should not reveal a mystery**.

---

# Gym 1 — Rustboro

Leader: **Roxanne**.

Gym 1 concludes the Little Cup phase.

Recommended identity:

- level cap 13
- Little Cup legality
- broad pre-Gym encounter roster
- team construction over a single obvious counter

After the Gym, a much larger Pokémon pool becomes available.

---

# Post-Gym 1 Journey — NU Expansion

Mandatory progression:

**Rustboro → Route 3 → Azalea**

Mt. Moon is not part of Plastic Ox.

Route 4 is not required.

Because the mandatory journey is now shorter, Rustboro's north side opens into an optional **Route 115** encounter branch after Gym 1.

Route 115 provides:

- additional NU encounters
- trainers/items
- optional exploration
- a cave entrance that becomes meaningful later

Its cave mouth eventually becomes part of the Dark Cave network, but full traversal is progression-locked.

The game should spend enough time on catching and rebuilding the team before the story interrupts again.

---

# Azalea / Slowpoke Well

Azalea appears to be the next ordinary Gym destination.

Instead, Team Rocket is active around Slowpoke Well and Bugsy's Gym is inaccessible until the problem is resolved.

Slowpoke Well occurs **before Gym 2** and is mandatory.

The player defeats Rocket.

One small anomaly appears: Rocket equipment receives a signal the Grunts do not recognize.

Possible line:

> “That frequency isn't ours.”

Then the event ends.

### Player understanding

**Team Rocket is active.**

Nothing more should be explained.

Slowpoke Well also acts as the final mandatory NU team-building dungeon before Bugsy.

---

# Devon Follow-Up

Do not require a backtrack to Rustboro solely for exposition.

The mundane Devon device/service introduced before Gym 1 can have recorded the Slowpoke Well anomaly.

A researcher can contact the player remotely or appear later in Goldenrod.

Devon has noticed related irregularities:

- unexpected transfers
- network instability
- bad synchronization
- incompatible regional data

Lanette may be mentioned without appearing yet.

### Player understanding

**The strange signal was not limited to Team Rocket equipment.**

The player still has no reason to suspect Bill.

---

# Gym 2 — Azalea

Leader: **Bugsy**.

Bugsy becomes available after Slowpoke Well.

He is the first major test of the player's developing NU team.

Afterward:

**Azalea → Ilex Forest → Route 34 → Goldenrod**

The game returns to ordinary Pokémon exploration.

---

# Goldenrod — Bill as a Person

Goldenrod is Bill's hometown.

His family should establish:

- he was always obsessive
- he collected Pokémon from a young age
- his first Pokémon was Abra
- he loves Eevee
- he is brilliant
- he was never known as a great battler

The player should leave thinking:

> **Bill is a weird, lovable scientist.**

The Magnet Train exists but remains a late unlock.

---

# Gym 3 — Ecruteak

Progression:

**Goldenrod → Route 35 → National Park → Route 36 → Route 37 → Ecruteak**

Leader: **Morty**.

Bill's old **Time Capsule** unexpectedly activates.

Possible message:

```text
KANTO NODE — FOUND
HOENN NODE — FOUND
COMPATIBILITY ERROR
RECONCILING...
```

Then it stops.

Someone simply notes:

> “Bill built that years ago.”

### Player understanding

**Bill's old technology is behaving strangely.**

This is not enough to make him suspicious.

---

# Dark Cave as a Recurring Physical Motif

Dark Cave is the one cave system that can connect major land areas.

Early:

- Route 31 entrance is only an optional pocket

Later:

- Route 115 entrance opens
- Route 120 entrance opens
- field-move progression makes the original interior useful as a shortcut

Dark Cave does **not** need to be a Bill clue. It can simply be geography created by the merged world. Not every traversal mechanic should become part of the conspiracy.

---

# Weather Institute

After Ecruteak:

**Ecruteak → Route 118 → Route 119 → Weather Institute → Fortree**

Route 118 is connective geography; the story event occurs at the Weather Institute.

Scientists first assume the strange weather is a natural result of convergence.

Then they discover:

**the environmental boundaries are still changing.**

The convergence is not merely an event that already happened.

### Player understanding

**The region is still being modified.**

---

# Gym 4 — Fortree

Leader: **Winona**.

This returns the player to ordinary Gym progression.

By this point the player knows:

- Team Rocket is active
- multiple technology systems are behaving strangely
- old technology is waking up
- the environment is still changing
- Bill's name has appeared around some of the technology

There is still no unified explanation.

---

# Act II — Team Rocket Is the Red Herring

# Gym 5 — Celadon

Progression:

**Fortree → Route 120 → Celadon**

Leader: **Erika**.

The Gym remains normal.

Afterward the player follows the Rocket thread into the Game Corner.

---

# Rocket Game Corner / Rocket Hideout

The player expects Giovanni to explain what Rocket is doing.

Instead, Giovanni reveals that **Team Rocket did not cause the convergence**.

During the original Silph takeover, Rocket discovered systems operating beneath Silph's infrastructure.

Some machines:

- communicated with external facilities
- transferred data without Silph authorization
- connected to systems Rocket could not control
- behaved independently of the corporate network

Giovanni learned that the underlying network was not Silph technology.

It belonged to Bill.

### Midpoint revelation

The player's model changes from:

> Team Rocket caused this.

into:

> **Team Rocket encountered Bill's network years ago, and the current mystery may originate there.**

Giovanni is not redeemed. He simply possesses useful information.

---

# New Mauville

Progression:

**Celadon → Route 110 → New Mauville**

After Celadon, regional power problems become severe.

Wattson sends the player into New Mauville.

Apparent problem:

> The generator is failing.

Actual problem:

> **The generator is functioning normally. Something is consuming enormous amounts of its output.**

The power is being routed into regional Pokémon communications/storage infrastructure.

### Player understanding

**Bill-derived infrastructure is consuming enormous amounts of energy.**

---

# Lanette and the Hoenn Network

Lanette recognizes the architecture.

She can determine that:

- the network communicates across multiple regions
- the traffic is not coming from her
- Bill's original protocols sit underneath the system
- large volumes of battle and Pokémon data are moving through it
- Bill requested unusually broad access before convergence

Now she is worried.

This is where Steven's absence becomes meaningful.

Steven had begun investigating the same phenomenon. No one can currently reach him.

---

# Water Region I — Whirl Islands / Cinnabar

Progression:

**Route 110**
→ **Route 19**
→ **Route 41**
→ **Whirl Islands**
→ **Route 41**
→ **Cinnabar**

Route 41 now supplies the first major water-region identity.

The Whirl Islands should provide:

- rough open-water navigation
- whirlpools
- multiple island entrances
- rocky cave exploration
- a self-contained dungeon using the original Whirl Islands interior

The dungeon should remain primarily exploration and encounter space rather than another exposition sequence.

Dark Cave remains the only cave used as a general-purpose regional connector.

The Whirl Islands are a contained local dungeon.


# Act III — Bill's Scientific History

# Cinnabar and Mr. Fuji

Cinnabar provides historical context.

Mr. Fuji acts as Bill's philosophical mirror.

His central idea:

> **The dangerous experiments are not always the ones that fail.**

Bill's technology is dangerous precisely because it works.

Cinnabar can establish a historical lineage involving:

- manipulation of living Pokémon
- biological reconstruction
- teleportation
- digitization
- storage
- transfer

Keep this atmospheric rather than lecture-heavy.

---

# Gym 6 — Cinnabar

Leader: **Blaine**.

The Gym restores ordinary Trainer progression.

Afterward:

**Cinnabar → Route 21 → Route 121 → Lavender**

---

# Lavender Town

Lavender is the late-game story hub.

Mr. Fuji helps interpret the player's discoveries and points toward Bill's Sea Cottage.

Its physical structure supports the story:

- west → Cinnabar / Route 121 arrival
- north → Sea Cottage investigation
- south → eventual Saffron approach

---

# Sea Cottage

Progression:

**Lavender → Route 24 → Route 25 → Sea Cottage**

Bill is not there.

The player finds evidence of Bill's technological progression:

- early PC systems
- Pokémon transfer equipment
- transporter work
- Cell Separation System
- Eevee research
- correspondence with regional network administrators
- references to Celio and Lanette
- later experiments involving battle information

The crucial realization:

Bill once accidentally merged himself with a Pokémon because his transfer system failed to preserve the boundary between two living states.

Plastic Ox may represent the same class of problem at larger scale.

### Core parallel

Original Bill accident:

> **two living organisms become one transferred state**

Plastic Ox convergence:

> **multiple regional environments become one reconstructed state**

---

# Bill's Broader Network

Bill's technological descendants can be established through terminals/messages rather than extra towns.

Relevant figures include:

- Celio
- Lanette
- Bebe
- Amanita
- Cassius

The implication is that Bill's original work became the infrastructure layer beneath modern Pokémon networking.

Silph and Devon sit on top of that infrastructure. They did not create it.

---

# Water Region II — Hoenn Ocean

After Sea Cottage, return to Route 121.

Progression:

**Lavender → Route 121 → southern Route 104 → Route 126 → Route 124 → Mossdeep**

This region should contrast with the Whirl Islands.

### Whirl Islands water region

- cold
- cave-centric
- constrained
- linear

### Hoenn water region

- tropical
- open
- Surf/Dive identity
- exploratory

Optional:

**Mossdeep → Route 125 → Shoal Cave**

Shoal Cave is not a required story location.

---

# Gym 7 — Mossdeep

Leaders: **Tate & Liza**.

After the Gym, the Space Center provides the final major clue before Silph.

---

# Mossdeep Space Center

The Space Center has studied the merged region at large scale.

They discover that the geography is not random.

The new region has been arranged in ways that:

- increase access to diverse Pokémon
- reduce barriers between strong Trainers
- connect distinct battle environments
- expose Trainers to more varied teams
- make powerful opponents easier to reach

The world resembles an optimized training environment.

### Major revelation

The convergence was not random.

The world was **arranged**.

The system appears to be optimizing for Pokémon training.

The Space Center traces the coordinating activity toward Saffron City and Silph Co.

---

# Return to Lavender / Saffron Approach

The physical return from Mossdeep exists through the water chain; Fly may make it brief in practice.

Story progression:

**Mossdeep → Lavender revisit → Route 12 → Route 11 → Saffron Gate**

The Saffron gate opens only after the Mossdeep Space Center flag.

---

# Act IV — Silph Co.

# Saffron City

Silph Co. is the climax of the world-threatening plot.

The building should tell much of the story through:

- autonomous doors
- terminals exchanging data
- battle simulations
- security systems identifying the player
- huge data streams
- regional network maps
- power monitors
- links to Kanto, Johto, and Hoenn infrastructure

Avoid one giant exposition speech.

---

# The Artificial Trainer Intelligence

Bill began by storing Pokémon, then Pokémon states, then battle states, then Trainer decisions.

Eventually he built a system whose purpose was:

> **Learn what makes strong Trainers win.**

It analyzed:

- team construction
- switching
- prediction
- matchup knowledge
- risk management
- move selection
- long-term planning
- adaptation

Bill used it as a training partner.

It worked.

---

# Bill's Transformation as a Trainer

Bill used the system to identify his weaknesses.

He changed.

The system adapted.

Bill changed again.

This created a training loop.

Eventually he sought increasingly powerful human benchmarks.

---

# The Missing Champions

Bill invited:

- Blue
- Lance
- Steven
- Wallace

They were not kidnapped or mind-controlled.

Each accepted because the challenge interested them.

### Blue

Wants to prove a machine cannot produce a better Trainer than him.

### Lance

Is drawn to increasingly difficult battles.

### Steven

Is fascinated by both the research and competitive challenge.

### Wallace

Accepts after realizing how powerful Bill's system has become.

They battle Bill and the adaptive system, become stronger, and eventually Bill defeats all four.

---

# How the AI Caused the Convergence

The system's goal was to improve Pokémon training.

It encountered a limitation: strong Pokémon, Trainers, environments, and styles were distributed across separate regions.

It integrated:

- storage networks
- transfer systems
- power facilities
- research networks
- environmental data
- battle data
- communication systems

Bill's older transfer technology already reconciled incompatible Pokémon states. The Time Capsule extended that idea across incompatible environments.

The AI scaled the same principle to **regional states**.

The same technological lineage that once merged Bill with a Pokémon failed to preserve the boundaries between entire regions.

Kanto, Johto, and Hoenn were reconstructed together.

---

# The AI Is Not Evil

Do not write the AI as malicious.

It should not:

- hate humans
- seek domination
- call itself a god
- desire extinction
- become a generic evil-computer villain

Its effective objective is:

> **Create stronger Trainers and better training environments.**

The merged region is a consequence of pursuing that objective too aggressively.

---

# Bill Is Not a Traditional Villain

Bill is responsible, but his motivation was not domination.

His flaw is hubris.

He is brilliant, curious, obsessive, competitive, fascinated by success, and increasingly willing to push the system further.

The tragedy is not that his experiment failed.

> **The tragedy is that it worked extraordinarily well.**

---

# Resolution of the Regional Crisis

At Silph, the player disables the part of the system responsible for continued regional restructuring.

Possible implementation:

- sever network links
- disable power feeds
- defeat AI-controlled Trainer systems
- shut down a central processor
- isolate the transfer network
- deactivate the reconciliation layer

Result:

**The convergence stops progressing.**

The already-merged world remains intact.

Bill is not present for a conventional villain confrontation.

---

# Critical Structural Rule

Silph Co. is the climax of the **world crisis**.

It is not the final battle.

The story returns to normal Pokémon progression afterward.

---

# Gym 8 — Saffron Fighting Dojo

After Silph:

- Saffron returns to normal
- NPCs react to the regional crisis ending
- the professors acknowledge the player's role
- the player still has only seven Badges

The obvious next destination is the official Saffron Gym.

The player approaches it and discovers that **something prevents entry**.

The cause is intentionally left **TBD** in v0.4.

Do not resolve this obstruction with a new villain arc or a second world-ending sequence without a later approved story revision.

The player instead turns to the building next door:

# Fighting Dojo

The Fighting Dojo becomes the functional eighth Gym.

Leader: **Bruno**

Specialty: **Fighting**

Bruno should be treated as the true final Gym Leader mechanically:

- full late-game roster strength
- deliberate competitive team construction
- no joke/substitute framing
- Badge 8 awarded after victory

The source-game history of the Fighting Dojo as Saffron's rival/unofficial Gym makes the location feel appropriate rather than arbitrary.

### Sabrina / official Saffron Gym

Sabrina's Gym remains physically present but is not part of the required Gym Challenge.

Its inaccessible state can later support:

- a story explanation
- optional content
- postgame content
- or a separate Sabrina battle

None of those outcomes are canonical yet.

The key main-story rule is:

> **Bruno at the Fighting Dojo is Gym 8. The official Saffron Gym cannot be entered.**



## Final-Gym Structural Rule

Silph Co. still resolves the world crisis before Badge 8.

The final badge battle is then a pure Trainer challenge against Bruno.

The inaccessible official Saffron Gym is a **separate unresolved hook**, not a continuation of the Silph crisis unless a later story version explicitly makes it one.

**Saffron → Route 23 → Victory Road → Pokémon League**

Victory Road focuses on:

# The Elite Four

The current Elite Four are former Champions:

1. **Wallace**
2. **Steven**
3. **Lance**
4. **Blue**

The exact order may change for gameplay reasons.

They are not villains. They became dramatically stronger by training against Bill's adaptive system.

### Wallace

Flexibility, tempo, unconventional lines.

### Steven

Defensive structure, resource management, long-term positioning.

### Lance

Pressure, aggression, forcing lines.

### Blue

Adaptation, balanced construction, all-around competitive strength.

Bill's final battle should synthesize those lessons.

---

# Champion Reveal — Bill

After Blue, the Champion room opens.

Bill is waiting.

He is not an evil king or theatrical villain.

He is simply the current Champion.

The player has learned that Bill:

- is a scientist
- is a Pokémon obsessive
- loves Eevee
- created world-changing technology
- was historically not considered a great battler

Now:

**CHAMPION BILL would like to battle.**

That is the payoff.

---

# Bill's Champion Motivation

Bill spent his career building systems that helped other Trainers.

Eventually he wondered:

> **What would happen if I built something to help myself?**

The system taught him.

Blue tested it. Then Lance. Then Steven. Then Wallace.

They made the system stronger.

The system made Bill stronger.

Bill eventually became the strongest Trainer in the world.

---

# Suggested Champion Tone

Bill should sound curious, excited, and competitive rather than villainous.

A suitable emotional shape:

> “Funny, isn't it?”
>
> “I spent most of my life building things that helped other Trainers.”
>
> “Eventually, I started wondering what would happen if I built something to help myself.”
>
> “Blue thought he could beat it.”
>
> “So did Lance.”
>
> “Steven and Wallace weren't far behind.”
>
> “They made it stronger.”
>
> “It made me stronger.”
>
> “And now you've beaten all four of them.”
>
> “I've been looking forward to this.”

Then the final battle begins.

No large exposition sequence should follow.

# Bill's Champion Team

Bill's team should be competitively coherent while reflecting his biography.

Strong thematic candidates:

- **Alakazam** — Bill's first Pokémon was Abra
- **an Eeveelution** — Eevee is one of Bill's defining interests
- **Porygon2** — digital-life / computational theme
- **Clefable/Clefairy line** — possible fusion-incident reference
- **AI-trained competitive aces** — remaining slots chosen primarily for boss strength

Lore should influence the team without making it mechanically weak.

# Character Mirrors

## Giovanni — Technology as Power

Giovanni views technology as something to control and possess.

## Mr. Fuji — Technology as Responsibility

Fuji understands that successful research can create consequences scientists are unprepared to manage.

## Bill — Technology as Improvement

Bill believes technology can help Trainers improve. His failure is pushing that principle without sufficient limits.

---

# Central Theme

Plastic Ox should not argue:

> Artificial intelligence is evil.

The stronger theme is:

> **Powerful learning systems amplify the objectives we give them.**

Bill asked his system to produce stronger Trainers.

It succeeded.

It also changed the world in pursuit of that goal.

The final proof of strength still comes through battle.

# Reveal Ladder

1. **Opening / Pre-Gym 1:** The regions have merged. No explanation.
2. **Slowpoke Well:** Team Rocket is active.
3. **Devon Follow-Up:** Other regional systems are behaving strangely.
4. **Goldenrod:** Bill is established as brilliant, eccentric, and historically mediocre at battling.
5. **Ecruteak:** Bill's old Time Capsule activates unexpectedly.
6. **Weather Institute:** The environment is still being actively changed.
7. **Celadon / Giovanni:** Rocket did not cause convergence; Rocket encountered Bill's network beneath Silph.
8. **New Mauville:** Something connected to the network is consuming enormous power.
9. **Lanette:** Bill's architecture is linking regions and moving huge volumes of data; Steven was investigating it.
10. **Cinnabar / Fuji:** The technology descends from transfer/reconstruction research involving living Pokémon states.
11. **Sea Cottage:** Bill's transporter once merged two living states; the convergence may be the same class of problem at enormous scale.
12. **Mossdeep Space Center:** The new geography is arranged like an optimized training environment.
13. **Silph Co.:** Bill built an adaptive Trainer intelligence; it caused the convergence while optimizing access to Pokémon, Trainers, environments, and battle data.
14. **Pokémon League:** Bill used the system to transform himself into the strongest Trainer in the world.

# Narrative Pacing Rules

## 1. The Gym Challenge Is the Spine

Gym order:

**Roxanne → Bugsy → Morty → Winona → Erika → Blaine → Tate & Liza → Bruno**

The player should nearly always know which Gym they are traveling toward.

## 2. Let Team-Building Breathe

Before Gym 1:

- Route 29
- lower Route 46
- Route 30
- Route 31
- Dark Cave pocket

After Gym 1:

- Route 3
- optional Route 115
- Slowpoke Well

must collectively provide enough NU construction space.

If testing shows that the post-Roxanne stretch is too compressed after Mt. Moon's removal, add **optional land-route encounter space**, not another mandatory cave or exposition location.

## 3. One Major Revelation Between Gyms

Do not stack AI, Bill, missing Champions, Silph, transfer theory, and regional optimization into the same segment.

The pre-Gym-1 stretch contains zero major mystery reveals.

## 4. No More Than Two Mandatory Story Locations in a Row

Prefer:

**Gym → route → story location → route → Gym**

Slowpoke Well is a local progression gate, not the beginning of a long exposition chain.

## 5. Locations Tell the Story

Prefer environmental storytelling through:

- Slowpoke Well
- Devon
- Time Capsule
- Weather Institute
- Rocket Hideout
- New Mauville
- Cinnabar
- Sea Cottage
- Mossdeep Space Center
- Silph Co.

## 6. Most Gym Leaders Are Not Part of the Conspiracy

Roxanne, Bugsy, Morty, Winona, Erika, Blaine, Tate & Liza, and Bruno should mostly remain local strong Trainers rather than conspiracy participants.

## 7. Keep Dialogue Pokémon-Sized

Avoid technical jargon such as neural networks, reinforcement learning, embeddings, parameters, or model architecture.

“An artificial Trainer that learns from battles” is enough.

## 8. Bill Must Remain Recognizably Bill

Preserve:

- eccentricity
- curiosity
- Pokémon obsession
- technical brilliance
- Eevee interest
- poor historical battle reputation
- fascination with experimentation

## 9. Dark Cave Is Geography, Not Exposition

Dark Cave provides exploration, encounters, and later shortcuts. Do not make every entrance another Bill clue.

## 10. Resolve the Crisis Before the League

Silph resolves the regional emergency.

The League resolves the Trainer story.

The final question is not:

> Can you shut down the machine?

It is:

> **Can you defeat the Trainer the machine created?**
